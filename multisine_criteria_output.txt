================================================================================================
STAGE 1A.  E CRITERION,  tau = trace(Mt_w)/2,  AND THE E GAP
================================================================================================
  tau = trace(Mt_w)/2 = <E>_w/<E>_1 (energy identity);
  g := D_ratio - (2 tau - 1) = (lam_max - 1)(lam_min - 1):
  g > 0 puts BOTH eigenvalues of Mt_w on the same side of 1, and
  tau then decides which side -- so E_ratio > 1 iff g > 0 AND
  tau > 1.  tau > 1 holds in every case below (it is the
  positive |xdot|-energy correlation), so within this family the
  test reduces to the sign of g, as intended.

  |tau-EI| compares tau against energy_identity AS IS: its
  <E>_1 is a finite-window average, so the residual is window
  ergodicity (zero for the exactly periodic cases), not a
  failure of the identity.  |fixed| rescales that <E>_1 to the
  exact infinite-window value; it must be < 1e-10 and is.

  Findings on this set:
   - E_ratio > 1 NOWHERE: every case, including all the
     D ratio > 1 ones and the constrained optimum, LOSES on the
     E criterion (in this normalisation).  g < 0 throughout --
     the D gain never exceeds 2 tau - 1, so lam_min stays
     below 1 even though the product of eigenvalues beats 1.
   - sign(E_ratio - 2/3) vs sign(D - 8/9): agree in 10/10 cases.
   - vmin is the losing direction in NORMALISED (u, udot)
     coordinates (0 deg = u, 90 deg = udot).  At M = 1 it equals
     psi exactly: in these coordinates the losing direction IS
     the direction of x itself -- the combination the trigger
     watches is the one that loses information.  Across the
     other cases it ranges from ~18 to ~50 deg.
   - REMINDER: E_ratio, vmin and g are tied to the choice
     S = diag(M_1)^(-1/2); D ratio is not.

================================================================================================
STAGE 1B.  EVENT-BUDGET OBJECTIVE AT FIXED T AND FIXED Delta
================================================================================================
  N ~ T m / Delta and det I_ev ~ (T/Delta)^2 J_ev with
      J_ev := m^2 det M_w = det( <|xdot| phi phi^T>_1 ).
  J_ev is NOT scale-invariant (it grows like power^3), so every
  case is first rescaled to unit input power <u^2>_1 = 1; the
  factor is printed.  The M = 1 reference for each case is a
  unit-power single sine at that case's HIGHEST frequency --
  under fixed T and a power constraint m grows with frequency,
  so that is the fair single-tone competitor (a modelling
  choice: a band-edge reference, stated, not settled).
