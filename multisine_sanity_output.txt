  The whole relative error lives in the two bins straddling a
  turning point, and it is NOT a failure of the density -- it is
  quantisation of the level ladder against a fixed histogram bin.
  That bin spans |x| in [cos(7.5deg), 1] * C, a band of width
  0.0086 C, so it contains only 0.0086 C / Delta ladder levels;
  below a few levels per bin the bin count is an integer artefact
  of where the ladder happens to fall, not a density estimate.
  (The turning point also loses one event per half period outright:
  the top level is crossed going up, but coming down the trigger
  needs a FULL Delta from it, so that level is never revisited.)

  Read this as a status report, not a result:
   - at M = 1 all four columns coincide, as they must;
   - at M > 1 the fundamental-phase and analytic-phase zeta
     DISAGREE, and both disagree with the FIM-implied values, so
     the single-complex-number description does not survive
     intact.  Which convention (if either) is the useful one is
     exactly the question for the next stage;
   - |z| det and |z| lam also part company at M > 1, which is the
     sharpest sign of it: they are two readings of the same
     number only under the single-sine FIM structure.  'nan' means
     det ratio > 1, i.e. no real |zeta| reproduces it at all.

  Flagging one thing without chasing it, since this session
  stops here: det ratio comes out ABOVE 1 for three of these
  multisines -- event-based sampling more informative than
  periodic at equal N, which a single sinusoid can never do
  (1 - |zeta|^2 <= 1).  Checked against the analytic time-average
  FIM and at 4x grid density, so it is not a sampling artefact.
  That is the thing to go after in the reachable-set study.
